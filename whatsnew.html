Beta channel

Version 5.2

The short list: what’s new since the App Store release

  • Allarise is now free. Every feature is unlocked for everyone; purchases became an optional tip jar. Existing purchasers and early adopters keep everything and are never prompted.
  • Slept Through Today. The app tallies alarms that rang ten minutes with no dismiss or snooze, resets at local midnight, and catches up even if you never open the app. The integration (3.4.0+) exposes it as a Home Assistant dashboard sensor with an alarms attribute.
  • Automatic alarm consolidation. When you’ve slept through a set of alarms, missions consolidate into one set instead of stacking, notes merge into one page, and sleep sounds stop for good when an alarm fires instead of resuming after snooze.
  • App Persistence went three-way. New Dynamic mode keeps the app awake only while charging or near an alarm, with a one-time notice explaining it to existing installs.
  • Custom accent colors. Pick an accent per theme and per light/dark appearance; it applies app-wide.
  • HA alert media fixes. Images now load from plain-http Home Assistant hosts, and video failures are surfaced instead of silent.

Slept Through Today (Home Assistant), 2026-08-06

Allarise now keeps a running tally of the alarms you slept through today, an alarm that rang for ten minutes with nobody dismissing or snoozing it. It counts alarms, not rings, so an alarm you snoozed and then slept through is one; a late dismiss still counts, on the day the alarm rang. The count resets at local midnight and catches up on its own, so you do not have to open the app for a slept-through alarm to be recorded.

The app publishes that count, and the Home Assistant integration (3.4.0 and later) turns it into a Slept Through Today sensor on the dashboard, with an alarms attribute listing which ones (name, index and the time it rang). It is the record, not a replay: a slept-through alarm never re-runs its after-alarm notes, verse or weather later. Full detail, and an example automation, are on the Home Assistant reference.

Version skew is safe either way: an older app never publishes the count so the sensor stays unavailable, and an older integration simply does not create it. Nothing errors.

Multi-mission alarms

An alarm can now run up to five missions in a row before it will dismiss. Mix them freely: maths then a shake, or three rounds of the same thing. Set them up under Missions when editing an alarm.

Two new missions

  • Block Drop: clear falling blocks to shut the alarm up.
  • Meteor Defense: shoot down what is coming at you.

Both have easy, medium and hard difficulty settings.

Radio

Internet radio stations can now be used as an alarm sound and as a sleep sounds source. Add stations in Settings, then pick one anywhere a sound is chosen. If a station cannot be reached when the alarm fires, Allarise falls back to your normal alarm tone rather than staying silent.

Optional usage data and product updates

Two new settings, both off unless you turn them on, under Settings → Allarise Pro:

  • Usage Data & Crash Reports: anonymous. Helps find crashes that cannot be reproduced and shows which features actually get used. Nothing you typed is ever included.
  • Product Updates: occasional notifications about new features. Tapping one opens this page.

Full detail on both is in the Privacy Policy (sections 2.3 and 2.7). Declining either costs you nothing else in the app.

App Persistence is now three-way

App Persistence (the setting that keeps Allarise awake in the background) used to be on or off. It now has a third option, and everyone has been moved to it. You will see a one-time notice in the app explaining the change.

On: always ready. The app stays awake all night. Highest battery use.

Dynamic: ready when it matters. The app stays awake while your phone is charging and in the roughly 75 minutes before your next alarm, and sleeps the rest of the time. This is the new default.

Off: best battery. The app sleeps until you open it.

Your alarm rings on time in all three. The alarm is scheduled with iOS itself no matter which you pick. What changes is the quality of the wake-up: whether the app can use your custom volume, play a radio station rather than its own tone, and answer Home Assistant. Under Dynamic those depend on iOS waking the app, so treat them as usually available in those windows rather than guaranteed. Choose On if you need certainty. Change it under Settings → Reliability.

If you use Home Assistant: commands sent to a phone on Dynamic reach it while it is charging or close to an alarm, and not while it is asleep. Switch to On if you need the phone to answer at any hour. Details are on the Home Assistant reference.

Home Assistant: fewer surprises from automations

Nothing you have already built needs changing: every field and payload works exactly as it did. What changed is how the app handles a few awkward cases:

  • Give create_alarm an mqtt_id and re-running it updates that alarm instead of making another one. This is the answer to "Home Assistant keeps creating alarms".
  • A ceiling of 50 alarms created from Home Assistant. Past that, create_alarm declines rather than crowding out the alarms you set by hand. Delete the ones you do not need, or reuse an mqtt_id.
  • Out-of-range values are trimmed rather than obeyed: snooze length, snooze count and hold durations are held to what the app itself offers, and a days list that mixes names and numbers now keeps every day you listed.
  • The optional ts freshness stamp is honoured everywhere, including per-alarm dismiss, snooze, skip and enabled. If you do not send ts, nothing changes at all.
  • Alert link buttons open web addresses only (http / https). To launch another app, use a per-alarm App Link.

All of it is written up on the Home Assistant reference, along with a broker access-control example worth reading if anything besides your phone and Home Assistant shares your MQTT broker.
